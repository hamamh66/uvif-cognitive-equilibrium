\subsection{Biological and ecological systems}
Physiological feedback provides motivation for analysing how multiple processes maintain functional ranges \citep{billman2020homeostasis}. However, identifying opposing regulatory signals is insufficient to establish epistemic pull, computational drag, or action efficiency as distinct state-space fields. Nor should chronic dysfunction be classified as successful adaptive homeostasis simply because it persists. The physiological mechanisms and criteria of function must be established independently.

Ecological dynamics further illustrate why an operating regime must not be conflated with a stationary point. Oscillations may occur around an equilibrium without convergence to it; their stability depends on the model. In the classical undamped Lotka--Volterra example, closed orbits are not asymptotically attracting. Other ecological models may possess attracting cycles, multiple equilibria, or transitions to different regimes. Such behaviours call for analysis of invariant sets and disturbances, rather than treating all persistence as evidence of a stable equilibrium.

Ecological resilience research motivates this broader attention to dynamics \citep{folke2006resilience}. Translating its mechanisms into \uvif{} requires explicit mappings, and the explanatory value of the original ecological model remains the baseline against which the translation should be judged.

\subsection{Artificial and organizational systems}
Artificial agents provide a more direct setting because computation, sensing, action, and resource monitoring can be instrumented. Nevertheless, the agent's reserve balance, processing budget, and environment must be modelled together. An update that reduces a cost surrogate may still consume additional resources, and a nominally protective rule may prevent useful adaptation. Performance should therefore be measured at the whole-system level.

Organizations offer a further analogy through the allocation of limited capacity, preservation of expertise, and long-term planning. Their objectives may conflict across stakeholders, and institutional persistence is not necessarily a social benefit. Organizational decision structures involving AI already require attention to the allocation of human and automated responsibilities \citep{shrestha2019decision}. A \uvif{} application would need to expose those choices rather than conceal them behind a single organizational equilibrium.

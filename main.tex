\title{Variational Equilibrium as an Organizing Principle\\[0.2em]
  of Natural and Artificial Cognitive Systems:\\[0.4em]
  From Objective Maximization to Balanced Persistence}

\begin{abstract}
Cognitive systems---biological and artificial alike---are routinely modelled as
optimizers: agents that maximize a scalar objective such as reward, utility,
accuracy, or negative prediction error. This paper argues that maximization is
the wrong idealization for any cognitive system that must remain functional over
long horizons, and develops an alternative. We introduce the \textbf{Unified
Variational Intelligence Framework (\uvif{})}, in which cognition is characterized
not by convergence toward a fixed target but by the continuous, constrained
balancing of five competing forces: an \emph{epistemic pull} driving the
acquisition of understanding, a \emph{protective shield} preserving critical
internal states, a \emph{computational drag} enforcing resource realism, an
\emph{efficiency imperative} governing action under time pressure, and a
\emph{sustainability field} ensuring long-term viability. The organizing
principle is \emph{Variational Equilibrium} (\(\VEq\)): a dynamic, adaptive
operating manifold that neither freezes a system into rigidity nor dissolves it
into instability, but sustains purposeful coherence through time. We position
\uvif{} with respect to the theoretical frameworks closest to it in cognitive
systems research---the free-energy principle and active inference,
resource-rational analysis, allostatic regulation, and symbolic and hybrid
cognitive architectures---and identify what it adds: an explicit separation of
epistemic from pragmatic drives rather than their conflation in a single
functional, and the elevation of resource cost and long-horizon sustainability
from implementation details to first-class forces in the system's dynamics. The
framework is developed conceptually and illustrated across biological
regulation, ecological resilience, human cognition, artificial agents, and
organizational systems. We state its claims as hypotheses rather than theorems,
specify the empirical predictions that would discriminate it from competing
accounts, and set out the mathematical and experimental work required to test
them.
\end{abstract}
